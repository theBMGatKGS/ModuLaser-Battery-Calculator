Quick Start

Every document represents one (1) ModuLaser SenseNET network. Every sheet within a document represents one (1) power supply. A single power supply may serve more than one Cluster; different Clusters or Cluster segments must be powered by different circuits. The Cluster number is what ties modules together across sheets when a Cluster is distributed across multiple power supplies. A network has 1 Command Display Module, with up to 1 optional backup CDM for redundant SenseNET configurations.

A fully clean, valid calculation is required before a sheet's power supply/battery recommendations and Bill of Materials will generate. The Review Validation Error flags and Resolve any INVALID CALCULATION banner steps below cover the yellow Validation Error flags and the red INVALID CALCULATION banner, what triggers each, and how to fix them.

1
Find your way around the screen

The screen has six parts, top to bottom and left to right. Menu bar — File, Edit, View and Help hold every command. Quick row — the most-used buttons: ☰ (show/hide the sheet sidebar), Import .pl, + Add Location, Save As…, Print, the Auto-Save indicator and ◧ (show/hide the results panel). Project Settings bar — the project and network header; click its title to collapse it to a one-line summary. Sheet sidebar (left) — every power supply sheet, grouped by Area/Group, with a status dot. Sheet (center) — the selected power supply's name, location, settings and module table. Live results panel (right) — required Ah, PASS/FAIL, load breakdown, clusters and validation messages for the selected sheet. On a narrow window the sidebar and results panel collapse; open them with ☰ and ◧ or from the View menu.

2
Fill in Project Settings

Every document represents one ModuLaser SenseNET network. In the Project Settings bar fill in Project Name, Prepared By, Date, Network #, Building, and the Primary (and optional Backup) Command Display Location, and turn Auto-Save on if you want it. These carry into every printed page, the export files and the Cluster Validation Report. Click Project Settings to collapse the bar: it then shows a one-line summary of the project and the sheet settings most sheets use (Design Basis, standby/alarm, temperature, margin).

Overrides: Design Basis, Standby, Alarm, Ambient Temp. and Safety margin are set on each sheet. When a sheet differs from what most sheets use, it shows a blue Overrides project settings: … badge next to its name, gets a blue dot in the sidebar, and is listed under the bar (click a name to jump to it). An override is allowed — it just makes the difference easy to spot.

3
Add power supply sheets and use the sidebar

Each sheet = one power supply. Add one with + Add power supply at the bottom of the sidebar, + Add Location in the quick row, or Edit → + Add Location. Click a sheet in the sidebar to show it. Sheets are grouped by their Location (the Area/Group name; an Import .pl fills it from the PipeCAD floor). Each shows its required Ah and a status dot: green complete, amber needs entry (amber-outlined fields left), red error (an INVALID CALCULATION condition or a Validation Error; the red count shows how many), and an extra blue dot when the sheet overrides the project settings. At the top of the sheet fill in the Power Supply Name (e.g. PS-1) and Location (e.g. Riser Closet A); use Remove ⌫ on the sheet to delete it. New sheets start with no modules (02.00.004): an empty sheet shows a guide with two choices — Import .pl… to bring modules in from PipeCAD, or + Add Display / + Add Detector to add them by hand. An empty sheet shows Needs entry and INCOMPLETE until it has modules.

4
Pick a design basis

NFPA 72 Standard/Voice, NFPA 72 + NFPA 110 Generator Backup (Non-Voice/Voice), UFC 3-600-01 Hydro or FA/MNS, AWS, CAN/ULC-S524:2024 Standard/Voice, four BS 5839-1 categories (M/L, M/L + Generator, P Staffed, P Unstaffed), or Custom — sets Standby and Alarm duration. New sheets start on - Select - for Design Basis and Ambient Temperature: both are amber (needs entry) and the sheet stays INCOMPLETE until you choose them; nothing is de-rated meanwhile. Saved and legacy files keep their values. The generator-backup presets reflect a 4-hour standby basis where a code-compliant NFPA 110 generator serves as secondary power in place of the full 24-hour battery standby — confirm with your AHJ that a generator qualifies for your specific installation before relying on this basis. BS 5839-1 presets use that standard's own formula rather than the NFPA-72-style formula used everywhere else — its De-Rating factor is determined automatically (1 or 1.75, per Annex E's own rule) based on the selected battery, and the Safety Margin control locks to 25% automatically since that matches BS 5839-1's fixed aging factor. Category P (Unstaffed) is the one preset where Standby stays editable, capped at 72 hours. A new Ambient Temperature selector next to Alarm applies a de-rating multiplier when batteries won't sit at the standard 77°F/25°C — set per sheet, since different power supplies may be in different environments. When a design basis has a known code citation and the sheet's calculation is clean, a compliance statement citing the exact section/paragraph appears at the bottom of the sheet automatically.

5
Build each cluster

A Standard or Minimum Display starts a new cluster (numbered 01–63) and can host up to 8 Detectors. A Command Display works differently — it's network-wide rather than cluster-scoped (up to 2 per network), used for command/supervisory functions, and never hosts Detectors directly. Add Detectors and assign each to a cluster hosted by a Standard or Minimum Display. Detectors on a display-less sheet can reference clusters hosted on other sheets — and detectors on that same sheet sharing that same remote reference can still ribbon-connect to each other locally, forming their own portion of the cluster's ribbon topology (up to the usual 2-segment limit for that sheet's contribution), while linking back to the rest of the cluster over SNET+ rather than ribbon.

6
Address, Zone, Power, Circuit source

The module table uses compact labels so nothing truncates or wraps: Module Type shows DISP-MIN (Minimum Display), DISP-STD (Standard Display), DISP-CMD (Command Display) or DETECTOR; Cluster shows the two-digit cluster number (e.g. 01); Power shows Own circuit for an entry point or Ribbon Path for a module powered over the ribbon (open the dropdown, or hover, to see which entry point it follows). On narrow windows the table scrolls sideways instead of squeezing columns. Printouts and Word exports use the same labels.

Give each module an Address (a dropdown of 1–127, unique across the entire network — once claimed on any sheet, it's disabled everywhere else) and a Zone descriptor. Every module needs an address; leaving one blank keeps the sheet in INVALID CALCULATION until it's set. Displays are always entry points from a circuit. By default, Ribbon Assignment (the toggle next to "MASD Modules") is set to Auto — new detectors automatically ribbon-connect to the first entry point in their cluster with room, and once a cluster's ribbon capacity is full (4 followers per entry point, 2 entry points per cluster), the next detector automatically becomes a new entry point. Auto also keeps every ribbon-connected group of modules sitting in consecutive rows on the sheet, moving them together automatically as clusters change — nothing you've entered on a module is lost when it moves, since the row itself relocates rather than being recreated. Switch it to Off to assign Power manually instead — Auto will stop re-evaluating your choices and stop reordering rows while it's off, though a flag will still recommend moving ribbon-connected modules together if they end up scattered. Each entry point also gets an AUX Circuit # (AUX 01–04) — new entry points default to a circuit nobody else is using yet, and once a circuit is claimed by one entry point, it's locked out for every other entry point on that sheet — a sheet has at most 4 entry points, since there are only 4 physical circuits. If a 5th entry point needs one and all 4 are already taken, it's left unassigned with a flag calling it out — free up a circuit (consolidate a module onto ribbon) or move it to another power supply page. Enter Circuit source on every entry point (e.g., PS-1 AUX-2).

7
Choose Networked or Standalone addressing

Just below "Clusters supported" is a per-sheet Networked / Standalone toggle. Networked is the default (unchanged for every existing project) — Address is the 1–127 dropdown, unique across every Networked sheet (one SenseNET network). Standalone is for a sheet with no SenseNET pathway connecting it to anything else: Address becomes a 1–9 dropdown and is unique within that sheet only (02.00.004) — a Standalone sheet may reuse addresses used on any other sheet, Networked or Standalone, and is never flagged for that; two modules on the same Standalone sheet with one address are still flagged. Cluster identification is still required either way, and cross-sheet cluster referencing keeps working. Switching a sheet to Standalone clears any of its addresses above 9.

Auto addressing (02.00.005). New build — the first time modules arrive in an empty project (first .pl import, after Confirm Settings? → Confirm, or the first + Add Display / + Add Detector) — you're asked Networked or Standalone? (sets every sheet's mode), then Auto address? Yes numbers every module: Networked 1, 2, 3… in sheet order across all sheets (past 127 the next whole sheet moves to SenseNET network 2 and starts at 1, with a reminder that the new network needs its own infrastructure); Standalone from 1 on every sheet. No leaves addresses for you to enter. Existing project — a new .pl import, a new sheet, more modules on a sheet, or Import Calc → Merge sheets — one prompt offers three choices: Continue addressing per previous sheets (only the new modules: Networked continues after the last address on its SenseNET network, and a new sheet moves to the next network if it would pass 127; a new Standalone sheet starts at 1, new modules on a Standalone sheet continue on that sheet), Manually address (nothing automatic), or Re-auto address entire project. New sheets take the project's mode; in a mixed project they keep the default and the Mixed addressing banner applies. Module order, ribbon, AUX and cluster assignments never change; every automatic change is one entry in Edit → Address change log and undoes in one step (Undo bar or Ctrl+Z). Several quick adds give one prompt, and it never opens on top of another prompt.

Mixed sheets. When a project has both Networked and Standalone sheets, an amber Mixed addressing banner appears above the sheet with Make all Networked… and Make all Standalone…. Each asks for confirmation first (Cancel changes nothing) and offers Also re-address all modules: Networked numbers every module 1, 2, 3… starting on the first sheet and continuing through every sheet in order; Standalone starts at 1 on every sheet. Module order, displays/detectors, ribbon (Power) links, AUX circuits and cluster assignments are not changed — only the Address values. Without re-addressing, addresses are kept (Standalone still clears any above 9). Imported designs (Import Calc, Import project, .pl re-import) keep their addresses; if they contain duplicates under these rules, a notice lists them right after the import.

SenseNET networks: one network holds up to 127 modules. Each Networked sheet has a SenseNET network selector (1, 2, …) on its Addressing row, and the sidebar tags each sheet SN1, SN2…; a sheet always stays on one network. Addresses must be unique within a network, so different networks may reuse 1–127. When the networked modules pass 127 (adding modules, or Also re-address), whole sheets — last in sheet order first — move to the next network (2, 3…) and are addressed from 1 there, and you are told that a new network was added and to make sure the design includes the infrastructure for it (its own SenseNET wiring/pathway and connection to the command display/FACP). Re-addressing as Networked numbers 1–127 on SenseNET 1 in sheet order and continues from 1 on SenseNET 2. Imports keep each sheet's network (older files: network 1) and never move sheets on their own; an import over 127 offers Move overflow to a new SenseNET network. Print, Word and Excel settings show Networked · SenseNET n. The project file (.mlproj.json) records each device's SenseNET network, so the Field Report (02.00.002 or later) checks addresses per network and accepts designs over 127 modules split across networks. Logged and undoable: every unify / re-address, SenseNET network move and Standalone clear is written to Edit → Address change log… (saved with the project, including .mlproj.json) with each module's address and network before and after, and can be undone in one step with the Undo bar that appears above the sheet or Ctrl+Z (not while a field or dropdown has focus). Undo is cleared by your next manual address, mode or network edit, so it never overwrites later changes. If a network is over 127 (for example after undoing an automatic move), the sheet stays flagged and a banner offers Move overflow to SenseNET n; automatic moves only happen when the count rises past 127. Clear file also clears the log. Switching to Standalone lists any addresses above 9 that will be cleared before you confirm, and tells you which were cleared afterwards.

8
Fan speed and supply mode

Set each detector's fan speed from PipeCAD. Pick Edwards A/BPS or third-party (custom loads / export ModuLaser loads only). Third-party custom mode has its own Auto Battery Sizing toggle, independent from built-in mode's — it defaults to Off so you can enter the Ah rating from your manufacturer's spec directly, with Auto available if you'd rather have it computed from total load. Enter your battery's actual voltage and the calculator works out how many units in series reach the 24 VDC nominal system target.

9
AUX + battery size

Auto Battery Sizing (the toggle above Required Battery Capacity, default Auto) picks battery size, Power supply capacity, and Battery cabinet together from your total load — the smallest catalog size that reaches 1 supply when possible, switching to 10 A only if AUX draw needs it. Switch it to Off to set all three manually instead. Edwards AUX outputs are rated 3.0 A per circuit, 6.0 A total on a 6 A supply (APS6A/BPS6A) or 8.0 A total on a 10 A supply (APS10A/BPS10A). For 50 Ah and 65 Ah batteries, a Battery cabinet choice appears — Edwards 3-RCC7R (with 3-BATS shelf at 65 Ah) or Space Age SSU00507.

10
Review Validation Error flags

Yellow-highlighted Validation Error flags call out detector/display mismatches, ribbon-segment size limits, duplicate addresses, missing circuit sources, and AUX coverage. They don't block editing, but the power supply/battery recommendation section, the multi-supply recommendation, the Recommended Power Supply Arrangement, and the Bill of Materials are all withheld until every flag clears — including a missing fan speed selection. Resolve everything shown here before expecting a hardware recommendation at the bottom of the sheet.

11
Resolve any INVALID CALCULATION banner

Separate from — and more severe than — a Validation Error flag, a bold red "INVALID CALCULATION" banner at the top of a sheet means the numbers themselves can't be trusted. It fires on any of four conditions, each with a specific fix:

  • Required battery capacity exceeds 65 Ah (the largest available battery) — increase the battery size where possible, or split the location's clusters across additional power supply sheets using the Recommended Power Supply Arrangement below.
  • A detector has no fan speed selected — select every detector's fan speed from your PipeCAD calculation. The current draw shown is incomplete until every fan speed is set.
  • A module has no Address selected — pick an address (1–127) for every module on the sheet.
  • AUX current exceeds the hardware limit (3.0 A per circuit, or 6.0/8.0 A total) — redistribute entry points across more AUX circuits, or switch Power supply capacity to 10 A if that alone resolves it.

The banner — and the red border and diagonal watermark that come with it in print — clear automatically the moment every condition is resolved.

12
Read the live results panel

The right-hand panel updates as you type. Required capacity shows the Ah per supply in large type with a gauge: the solid part is the standby + alarm load, the lighter part is the safety margin and temperature de-rating, the blue mark is the selected battery and the dashed red mark is the 65 Ah maximum. Below it a status line reads PASS (valid, no flags, within the selected battery and 65 Ah), FAIL (an INVALID CALCULATION condition, more than one supply needed, or capacity beyond the battery / 65 Ah) or INCOMPLETE (Validation Error flags still open). Then the selected battery and recommended hardware, the load breakdown (modules, power supply quiescent + AUX, total, standby/alarm/required Ah), the clusters on this supply, and the sheet's own validation messages — red for INVALID CALCULATION, yellow for Validation Errors, amber for fields that still need entry (with Show next › to jump to each one). The full calculation, hardware and Bill of Materials stay on the sheet below the module table. Until a sheet has modules it shows Incomplete for battery capacity everywhere: Live Results, the sidebar, the sheet's results card, print, Word, Excel and the project file (required Ah left empty and marked incomplete). There's no Ah figure, no PASS, and no battery or power-supply recommendation.

13
Multi-supply arrangements

If more than one supply is required, check the Recommended Power Supply Arrangement and use "Export additional arrangements" to split them onto their own sheets. Cluster numbers stay the same across sheets — View → Cluster Report ties everything back together. Each exported arrangement becomes its own sheet in the sidebar.

14
Save your work and export results

Save As… (quick row, or File → Save As…) downloads the whole document — every sheet, every field — as a single self-contained .html file you can keep, email, or reopen later in any browser; opening it restores the document exactly as saved. The Auto-Save toggle (in the Project Settings bar; its state shows at the right of the quick row) is a convenience on top of that: once turned on, it saves your current work automatically to this browser's local storage as you go, so a reload doesn't lose it — but it's tied to this specific browser and device, not portable like a saved file, so it's not a substitute for an occasional Save As when you want a real, shareable copy. File → Export to Excel saves an .xlsx workbook with a CAD Schedule sheet and a Detail sheet (see Print or export, below) — useful for drawings and for anyone who wants the data outside this tool. Print / Export… offers PDF, Word and HTML as well.

15
Amber needs-entry highlights

Empty required fields — selections with no usable default, such as Power Supply Name, Location, a module's Address, Zone and Fan Speed — show an amber (orange) 2px outline and light amber tint so unfinished entry is easy to spot. This is intentionally different from the yellow Validation Error panel and the red INVALID CALCULATION banner. Amber clears when you fill the field. Valid defaults are never flagged (02.00.004): Design Basis, Standby (h), Alarm (min), Ambient Temp., Module Type, Power, Addressing, supply mode, NFPA 72 edition and margin keep their defaults without amber and never make a sheet Needs entry. Date is excluded: new and cleared documents still initialize Date to today, and you can edit it normally afterward, but it is never amber-highlighted. Project-header fields (Project Name, Prepared By, Network #, Building, CDM locations) are amber only while empty.

16
Confirm defaults

Since 02.00.004 you no longer need this step: valid defaults are not amber-flagged and never hold a sheet in Needs entry. Edit → Confirm defaults is kept (it is harmless — it only marks the current sheet's defaults as reviewed and changes no values). Empty required fields stay amber until you fill them.

17
Clear a sheet or the entire file

Edit → Clear… asks what to wipe. This sheet only resets the current power-supply sheet to a blank sheet (no modules, default settings) and leaves project header fields alone — you confirm before it runs. Entire file removes every sheet, then asks whether to keep or clear the project/header fields, and asks for a final confirm before wiping. Either path starts you with a fresh blank sheet afterward. (The separate Edit → Clear saved data only wipes browser Auto-Save storage — different from Clear….)

18
Import Calc from a saved .html

File → Open / Import Calc… opens a file picker for a calculator .html that was produced with Save As… (those files embed the document data needed for import). If the file's revision is older than this app, you get a warning and can cancel or continue. Then choose Replace document (swap your current header + sheets for the imported file) or Merge sheets (add the imported power-supply sheets into the current file). On merge, if an imported sheet name collides with one you already have, you pick per collision: Skip, Keep both (auto-rename), or Overwrite existing.

19
Find commands in the menu bar and quick row

The menu bar at the top holds every command. File: New…, Open / Import Calc…, Import .pl…, Linked .pl files…, Import project…, Export project…, Save As…, Export to Excel, Print / Export… (Ctrl+P) and the Print BOM switch. Edit: + Add Location, Clear…, Confirm defaults, Clear saved data. View: Cluster Report, Project Settings bar, Sheet sidebar, Results panel. Help: Help, How Do I…, Revision log. The quick row underneath holds ☰ (sheet sidebar), Import .pl, + Add Location, Save As…, Print, the Auto-Save indicator and ◧ (results panel). Menus work from the keyboard too: Tab to a menu name, press Enter or ↓ to open it, ↑/↓ to move, ←/→ to switch menus, Esc to close.

20
Import PipeCAD .pl files

File → Import .pl… (or Import .pl in the quick row) opens a four-step wizard. 1 Battery settings — set Design Basis, standby/alarm, ambient temperature, safety margin, NFPA 72 edition, power-supply capacity and supply mode first; they become the defaults on every new sheet and drive the 65 Ah check. 2 Pick files — drop or choose one or more .pl files; each shows its project (the file name is used when ProjectName is blank), units (metric files are converted to feet), Areas/Groups, detectors, displays and PSU positions. Untick any Area/Group you don't want. Unknown detector types or display part numbers are listed and skipped. 3 Rename — set a naming pattern from {project} {file} {area} {name} {##} (default {project}-{area}-Det{##}, remembered for next time), edit any zone or Area/Group name, and watch the live character counter. The ModuLaser display shows 16 characters total, including spaces, so names over 16 are blocked until shortened — use the suggested short name or Shorten all over 16. Duplicate names are outlined in red. 4 Power supplies — each Area/Group gets at least one supply, a supply never spans Areas/Groups, and each takes at most 4 detectors and 1 display with the required battery ≤ 65 Ah (display load included). Suggestions start from PSU positions in the file (nearest device first) and devices within 3 ft of each other in plan view (X/Y); oversize groups are split by distance. Any device that isn't near a file PSU or within 3 ft of another device is not auto-assigned — it waits in that Area/Group's Unassigned — group these to a power supply tray, and you're prompted to group it: drag it onto a supply card or use Move to…, and click + Add power supply for more supplies (removing a supply sends its devices back to the tray). Pack into fewest is an optional shortcut that assigns the whole Area/Group to the fewest supplies. Create sheets stays disabled, showing how many detectors are still unassigned, until every device is on a supply and every rule passes. One sheet is created per supply, with type, zone name, fan speed and display model filled in, its Location set to the Area/Group (so the sidebar groups them), and addresses and anything the file doesn't provide highlighted for you to enter — the sidebar shows those sheets with an amber needs entry dot until you finish. Design Basis and Ambient Temp. must be chosen in step 1 (they start on - Select -). After Create sheets a Confirm Settings? prompt summarizes the settings applied: Confirm marks everything configured during the import as confirmed, so only genuinely empty fields (e.g. addresses) stay amber; Revise import undoes the import and returns you to the review step with your choices kept. After Confirm you're asked how to address the new modules (a new build: Networked or Standalone? then Auto address?; an existing project: Continue / Manually address / Re-auto address entire project — see Addressing).

21
Keep linked .pl files up to date

Every imported .pl stays linked in the calc file: its name, size, last-modified date, SHA-256 fingerprint, and a fingerprint for each detector mapped to its sheet and row. File → Linked .pl files… lists them. Check link… asks you to pick the current copy of the .pl (a browser can't re-open a file on its own). If it changed, a banner appears above the sheets with the number of added, removed and changed devices. Review & re-import… shows the differences: fan-speed and name updates are pre-ticked only where you haven't edited that row by hand, removed devices are only deleted if you tick them, and new devices go through the rename step, then land in the power-supply step's Unassigned tray for you to group. Addresses are never changed.

22
Print or export (PDF, Word, HTML, Excel)

File → Print / Export…, Print in the quick row, or Ctrl+P opens the Print / Export dialog with a live 8.5 × 11 in preview. Under Sheets choose All power supply sheets or Current sheet only; pick a Detail level; under Include tick Project summary page and Bill of Materials (the same setting as File → Print BOM); then under Save as click a format:

  • PDF — opens the print window; choose “Save as PDF”, Letter. In the Windows app the PDF is written directly and you pick where to save it.
  • Word (.docx) — built in the app, offline, with the same pages, tables and graphics.
  • HTML — one self-contained .html report you can email or open in any browser; Full detail uses expandable sections.
  • Excel (.xlsx) — the CAD Schedule and Detail sheets (see the next step). File → Export to Excel saves the same workbook directly.

Page 1 is the project summary: project header and one row per power supply with Area/Group, required Ah, battery and PASS/FAIL. Then each power supply gets one letter page: header (project, PSU tag, location/Area-Group, calculator revision), the capacity gauge, a PASS/FAIL stamp, a mini cluster diagram (the supply with its displays ■ and detectors ○ labelled with fan speed), the module table (Zone, Type, Model, Cluster, Power, Fan, Addr, Standby mA, Alarm mA, with totals and Required Ah | Battery | status), the settings used, any validation messages, the BOM (if included) and the code-compliance statement when the sheet is clean.

23
Choose a detail level

The Detail level in the Print / Export dialog controls how much of the calculation goes into PDF, Word, HTML and the Excel Detail sheet (the preview updates live):

  • Summary — results only: the one-page sheet per power supply.
  • Standard — adds the formulas with your values filled in (standby, alarm, margin, temperature, battery and supplies). If a sheet would overflow its page, the formulas move to an appendix page right after it.
  • Full — adds appendix pages after each sheet with every step: per-module currents, subtotals, quiescent and AUX load, standby/alarm Ah, the BS 5839-1 de-rating factor D where it applies, safety margin, temperature multiplier, battery selection, supplies required and the BOM. Each step shows the formula, the formula with values substituted, the result with units, and its reference — the Design Basis citation the sheet uses (e.g. NFPA 72 (2022 Edition), Section 10.6.7.2 or BS 5839-1:2025, Annex E), or the calculator's own data table where that is the source.

Every number is read from the sheet's own calculation, so exports always match the screen. The on-sheet Show granular calculation detail checkbox still shows the same breakdown on screen.

24
Paste the CAD Schedule onto a drawing

The Excel workbook's CAD Schedule sheet has one compact table per power supply — a row with that sheet's Standby time (h), Alarm time (min) and Safety margin (%) (02.00.005), then Zone, Type, Fan speed, Address, Standby mA, Alarm mA, then Total load and a footer row Required Ah | Battery | PASS/FAIL — with thin plain borders, fixed column widths, no merged cells and no fills. To place one on a drawing: select that table's cells in Excel, copy, then in AutoCAD use Paste Special → AutoCAD Entities (a native table) or Picture (Metafile). The Detail sheet holds the full calculation at the chosen detail level, grouped per power supply and per step — use Excel's outline buttons (1 / 2 / 3 or + / −) on the left to expand or collapse it.

25
Share the design with the Field Report (project file)

File → Export project… downloads a .mlproj.json ModuLaser project file. It holds the project details, every power supply sheet (name, Area/Group, model, battery model, Ah and quantity, required Ah, standby and alarm time, PASS/FAIL), every detector and display (16-character name, type, address, power supply, fan speed) and, for detectors imported from PipeCAD, the pipe data commissioning needs: each sampling hole's size, flow, transport time and sensitivity, test points, pipe length, total flow, hole balance, fittings, Alarm Factor and design sensitivity, plus the .pl PSU and Battery positions. Open it in the MASD Field Report with File → Import project… and every detector, display and power supply sheet is created for you; nothing is typed twice. Both tools save the same file: the calculator owns the design part and the Field Report owns the site results. File → Import project… here opens a project file; if sheets are already on screen it lists every difference (added, removed or changed devices and power supplies) before replacing them. The Field Report's site results are never edited by the calculator; they are kept and written back unchanged the next time you export. Set addresses here: the Field Report imports them and confirms them on site.

26
Add a MiniLaser (standalone ASD, FHSD8075-ULF)

Each sheet has a Standalone ASDs (MiniLaser FHSD8075-ULF) table below the MASD modules. + Add MiniLaser opens the Pre-engineered picker (MiniLaser manual ch.5, Tables 13–31): choose T- or I-shaped, fan speed, number of holes and alarm factor — only combinations in the manual's tables are offered — and it fills the fan speed, alarm factor and DIP settings and shows pipe length, hole diameters, transport time and average sensitivity. Cancel switches the row to Manual config for non-pre-engineered installs; the fan speed stays blank and amber (⚠) until you choose it. A MiniLaser is never addressed and never on SenseNET; it needs its own AUX circuit (one MiniLaser per circuit, never shared) with the same 35 mA per-circuit overhead and 3.0 A per-circuit limit. Draw by fan speed 1–10: 120/134/157/180/200/230/255/285/320/365 mA (standby = alarm). Pick 0–2 I/O Expansion Modules (9-30521); the 9-30520 housing is added to the BOM automatically. A .pl import brings SOLO detectors in here with the fan speed and alarm factor from the .pl; when the .pl has no alarm factor the highest factor whose average hole sensitivity (Fire 1 × holes) stays within the class limit is used, and DIP or Remote configuration is recommended.

Standards and Formulas

MiniLaser (02.01.000). General reference only — not a substitute for the current adopted edition of NFPA 72, your local building/fire code, the AHJ's requirements, or the equipment manufacturer's published installation instructions. Code editions and local amendments change; always verify against what's actually adopted for your jurisdiction and project.

Every Design Basis preset in this calculator resolves to one of the battery-sizing formulas below. This section names the reference standard behind each preset and shows the exact formula used, so the math can be checked against the source document directly.

NFPA 72 (2022 Edition), Section 10.6.7.2

Governs the Standard, Voice, and both NFPA 110 Generator Backup presets. Also the underlying formula for AWS and both UFC 3-600-01 presets — see below.

Required Capacity (Ah) = (Istandby × Tstandby + Ialarm × Talarm) × 1.25

Tstandby = 24 h (or 4 h where an NFPA 110-compliant generator serves as secondary power) · Talarm = 5 min (standard) or 15 min (voice/mass notification) · 1.25 = the standard's aging/safety factor for the 2022 edition and later (editions before 2022 used 1.20 — this calculator's NFPA 72 Edition selector reflects that difference as the minimum Safety Margin). This 1.25 aging factor is mandatory (NFPA 72-2025 § 10.6.7.2.14) — it is separate from temperature effects, which NFPA 72 does not reduce to a single mandatory sizing multiplier; see the FAQ entry on the Ambient Temperature selector for the full distinction.

UFC 3-600-01, Fire Protection Engineering for Facilities (8 August 2016, w/ Change 6 dated 6 May 2021)

Uses the same NFPA 72 formula above, with its own standby/alarm durations specified for two distinct scenarios:

Required Capacity (Ah) = (Istandby × Tstandby + Ialarm × Talarm) × 1.25

Hydro (¶4-24.7): Hydroelectric Power Plant voice evacuation — follows NFPA 72's in-building voice evacuation provisions: Tstandby = 24 h, Talarm = 15 min. FA/MNS (¶9-18.11.3): Fire Alarm and Mass Notification System — Tstandby = 48 h, Talarm = 15 min.

AWS Specification 283116, 25H1B

Not a distinct sizing standard of its own — AWS cites NFPA 72's own requirements (Section 10.6.7.2, same formula as above), with the Owner specifying an enhanced alarm period beyond the code minimum:

Required Capacity (Ah) = (Istandby × Tstandby + Ialarm × Talarm) × 1.25

Tstandby = 24 h (NFPA 72 code minimum, unchanged) · Talarm = 120 min — an Owner-driven enhancement in place of NFPA 72's 5/15-minute code minimum, reflecting mission-critical reliability expectations beyond baseline code requirements. Also compliant with NFPA 855.

CAN/ULC-S524:2024

Canadian standard for fire alarm system installation. Structurally the same formula shape as NFPA 72, with its own 1.25 safety factor introduced specifically in the 2024 edition (not present in earlier editions in the same form). Not yet adopted into the National Building Code of Canada as of this writing, though already referenced by the Ontario Fire Code — confirm what's actually adopted in your jurisdiction.

Required Capacity (Ah) = (Istandby × Tstandby + Ialarm × Talarm) × 1.25

Tstandby = 24 h · Talarm = 5 min (Standard) or 15 min (Voice, where voice communication is installed) · 1.25 = the fixed safety factor this calculator applies for both presets (shown via the Safety Margin selector, locked to 25% while a CAN/ULC-S524:2024 preset is active — this calculator does not have independent confirmation of a distinct margin requirement in editions before 2024, so it doesn't attempt to model one).

BS 5839-1:2025, Annex E

A materially different formula from NFPA 72 — applies a derating factor (D) to the alarm term specifically, rather than one factor uniformly. Governs all four BS 5839-1 presets in this calculator.

Cmin = 1.25 × (I1 × T1 + D × I2 × T2)

I1×T1 = standby term · I2×T2 = alarm term · D = 1 or 1.75, determined automatically by comparing the selected battery's 20-hour rate to the alarm current (see the De-Rating factor field and the Battery and Charger Sizing entry below for the full determination rule) · 1.25 = fixed aging factor (shown via this calculator's Safety Margin, locked to 25% while a BS 5839-1 preset is active). Standby/alarm durations by category: M (Manual) or L (Life Safety) = 24 h / 30 min · M/L with generator = 6 h / 30 min · P (staffed/ARC-monitored) = 24 h / 30 min · P (unstaffed) = 24 h + unoccupied period, up to 72 h total / 30 min.

Other Code Requirements
Listing requirements

ModuLaser requires "UL Listed," "FM Approved," or other NRTL-specific certification depending on the requirements of the local authority having jurisdiction (LAHJ) — the acceptable framework isn't the same everywhere. Consult the ModuLaser manual for your region to determine which certification framework applies before selecting a power supply.

Compatibility listing — UL regions

In UL regions, any power supply listed under category UOJZ or UTRZ is suitable for use with ModuLaser, provided both the power supply and the ModuLaser modules are installed and configured strictly in accordance with each manufacturer's published instructions. Deviating from either manufacturer's published instructions — even with a UOJZ/UTRZ-listed supply — can invalidate the installation's compliance.

Reserve capacity (secondary power / battery standby)

NFPA 72 requires secondary power (typically batteries) sized to supply the system for a specified standby period, then still carry the full alarm load for a specified alarm period — commonly 24 hours standby + 5 minutes alarm for a standard system, or 24 hours + 15 minutes where voice/mass notification is involved. This calculator's Design Basis presets encode exactly these standby/alarm combinations. Where a code-compliant emergency or standby generator serves as secondary power in place of full battery standby, NFPA 110 governs the generator itself and the standby duration may be reduced (commonly to 4 hours) — the NFPA 72 + NFPA 110 Generator Backup presets reflect this, but confirm with your AHJ that a generator is accepted as secondary power for your specific occupancy and installation before relying on the reduced duration.

Battery and charger sizing margin

Both standards this calculator supports apply a similar aging/safety margin, typically 20% to 25% of the raw standby + alarm Ah calculation: NFPA 72 uses 1.25 (25%) in the 2022 edition and later, or 1.20 (20%) in editions before 2022 — this calculator's NFPA 72 Edition selector sets the applicable minimum accordingly. BS 5839-1:2025 uses a fixed 1.25 (25%) aging factor, accounting for roughly 5% capacity degradation per year over a 4-year assumed battery life. This calculator's Safety Margin selector applies the percentage on top of the raw Ah requirement (locked to 25% and disabled while a BS 5839-1 preset is active, matching its fixed factor); the AH per AUX Circuit and Total AUX Current checks separately confirm the supply's charger and output circuits aren't overloaded independent of battery sizing.

BS 5839-1:2025 battery de-rating factor (D)

In BS 5839-1:2025 Annex E (reorganized and clarified from Annex D/E in the 2017 edition — if updating from 2017, confirm your calculations use the new formula and D-factor application), the battery de-rating factor D accounts for reduced battery capacity when discharged at high alarm currents (the Peukert effect). It's applied in the standby battery capacity formula: Cmin = 1.25 × [(I1×T1) + D×(I2×T2)], where I1/T1 are the standby current/period, I2/T2 are the alarm current/period (typically 0.5 h), and 1.25 is the aging factor described above.

How D is determined: if the battery's 20-hour rate capacity (Ah rating ÷ 20, giving a sustained current in Amps) meets or exceeds the alarm current, D = 1. Otherwise, D = 1.75 (or a higher manufacturer-specified value). D = 1 is rare for fire alarm batteries in practice, since alarm loads are typically high relative to available battery capacity — D = 1.75 is the typical default for VRLA batteries in fire alarm systems. This calculator determines D automatically: it compares the currently selected battery's 20-hour rate against the sheet's total load (used as the alarm current in this calculator's model), and applies D = 1 only when that battery genuinely qualifies — otherwise D = 1.75. You can still manually enter a higher value if your battery or panel manufacturer specifies one, but the calculator won't let you enter a value below whichever the standard determines for the battery actually selected. Always confirm against the panel manufacturer's data and the battery manufacturer's specifications; failing to apply the correct D can lead to undersized batteries and non-compliance.

Circuit and output ratings

Every notification, initiating, and auxiliary output circuit on a listed power supply has a maximum rated current that must not be exceeded, and the supply's total output across all circuits combined has its own separate maximum. This calculator enforces the Edwards-published 3.0 A per AUX circuit and 6.0 A (6 A supply) / 8.0 A (10 A supply) total limits as part of its Validation Error checks — exceeding either is a real installation defect, not just a calculator formality.

Wiring and pathway survivability

Depending on occupancy type and the specific circuits involved, NFPA 72 may require initiating device circuits, notification appliance circuits, or signaling line circuits to use a specific pathway survivability level (Class N through Class X, per NFPA 72's pathway classification), which affects wiring topology, supervision, and fault tolerance — separate from, but related to, the ribbon/circuit source assignments tracked in this calculator's Power column and Cluster Validation Report.

Documentation for the AHJ

Most AHJs expect battery calculations, device compatibility, and circuit loading to be documented and available for plan review and final inspection. The printed sheet, Cluster Validation Report, and Bill of Materials this calculator produces are meant to support that documentation — but the calculator itself doesn't submit to or track approval from any AHJ, and its output should be reviewed by a qualified, licensed design professional before submission.

FAQs

Why doesn't a MiniLaser get an address?

The MiniLaser (FHSD8075-ULF) is a standalone aspirating detector with no SenseNET connection . It sits in each sheet's Standalone ASDs table, is left out of addressing, auto-addressing, duplicate checks and the 127-per-network count, and can go on any sheet, Networked or Standalone (02.01.000).

Why does each MiniLaser need its own AUX circuit?

Per Brent (2026-10-16): every MiniLaser is on its own dedicated AUX output, never shared with a ModuLaser entry point or another MiniLaser, with the same 35 mA per-circuit overhead and 3.0 A per-circuit limit. The Edwards supplies have 4 AUX circuits, so more MiniLasers plus entry points than 4 is a validation error that recommends adding a power supply.

Where does the MiniLaser alarm factor come from?

From the PipeCAD .pl when PipeCAD's Alarm Factor option was on. When it is off (alarmFactorActivated false), the calculator uses the highest alarm factor whose average hole sensitivity — detector Fire 1 level (AF 8 0.25, 7 0.18, 6 0.12, 5 0.08, 4 0.04, 3 0.03, 2 0.02, 1 0.01, 0 0.005 %obs/ft) × number of sampling holes — is at or below the class limit (VEWFD 1.0, EWFD 1.5, SFD 2.0, UL Open Area 2.18 %obs/ft), rounding toward more sensitive. If even 0 is over the limit it sets 0 and flags a validation error recommending fewer holes or a redesign. DIP switches offer only fan 2/4/7/10 and alarm factor 2/4/6/8; anything else needs Remote configuration (Remote.exe).

Why didn't my .pl MiniLaser expansion import?

Expansion items in a .pl are not imported (Brent 2026-10-16). Enter the number of I/O Expansion Modules (9-30521, 0–2) on the MiniLaser row; the import shows a notice when the .pl lists expansions.

What is the MASD Engineering Toolbox, and is the math the same?

The MASD Engineering Toolbox (00.01.000) absorbs this calculator and adds detector/display configuration, legacy-file migration and the field-change approval loop. Both apps run the same MASD engine (shown in Help → Revision Log → Calculation engine) and the same .pl import, so the same inputs give the same Ah, supplies and battery in both. Harmonization rule: any engine change bumps both apps' revisions with matching notes. Projects move between them through the same .mlproj.json file; this calculator keeps the Toolbox's configuration, field changes and approvals unchanged when it exports.

What do the sidebar's status dots mean?

Green: complete — no validation messages and no fields left to enter. Amber: needs entry — amber-outlined required fields are still empty, or the sheet has no modules yet (valid defaults are never flagged); click Show next › in the Live Results panel to jump to each one. Red: error — the sheet has an INVALID CALCULATION condition or a Validation Error (the red number is how many). A second, blue dot means the sheet overrides the settings most sheets use (Design Basis, standby, alarm, temperature or margin). The same legend is at the bottom of the sidebar.

How is PASS / FAIL / INCOMPLETE decided?

It only reads the sheet's own results. PASS: no INVALID CALCULATION condition, no Validation Error, one supply is enough and the required Ah per supply is within the selected battery and the 65 Ah maximum. FAIL: an INVALID CALCULATION condition, more than one supply required, or capacity beyond the selected battery or 65 Ah. INCOMPLETE: Validation Error flags are still open, so the recommendation and BOM are withheld. The status appears in the results panel, the summary page, each PSU page stamp and the CAD Schedule footer.

Why are Standby mA and Alarm mA the same in the module table?

This calculator uses one current per module (from the ModuLaser 24 V current-draw reference) for both standby and alarm — the same total load drives the Standby Ah and Alarm Ah lines. Both columns are shown because CAD schedules usually list both.

Do the exports need an internet connection?

No. PDF, Word (.docx), HTML and Excel (.xlsx) are all built inside the calculator file itself — nothing is downloaded from the internet, in the browser or in the Windows app.

Where did the toolbar go?

Every command is in the menu bar (File, Edit, View, Help); the most-used ones are also in the quick row under it. Print is now Print / Export…, with PDF, Word, HTML and Excel in one dialog.

How does Import .pl decide which detectors share a power supply?

PipeCAD's "Floor" level is treated as an Area/Group. Within each Area/Group, PSU positions saved in the .pl are used as the starting supplies and each device goes to the nearest one that still has room. Without PSU positions, detectors and displays within 3 ft of each other in plan view (X/Y, height ignored) are suggested together. A suggestion is split by distance if it would exceed 4 detectors, 1 display, or a required battery over 65 Ah using the step 1 battery settings, Edwards A/BPS quiescent load and one 35 mA AUX circuit. Devices more than 3 ft from everything else (and not placed on a file PSU) are left in the Area/Group's Unassigned tray for you to group — drag them onto a supply, use Move to…, add supplies, or use the optional Pack into fewest. Create sheets stays blocked until the tray is empty. Re-imported new devices also start in the tray. The sheet's own calculation is still the final word once it's created.

What's a "cluster"?

A Standard or Minimum Display Module (FHSD8310-ULF / FHSD8300-ULF) plus up to 8 Detector Modules (FHSD8330-ULF) wired to it. The display is what makes a cluster a cluster — detectors alone have nowhere to report.

What's the difference between Standard, Minimum, and Command Display?

Standard and Minimum Displays each host one cluster of up to 8 detectors. A Command Display is network-wide — it shows status across the whole network but doesn't host detectors itself, and only 2 are allowed per network.

What is a "network" in this calculator?

Each location page represents one ModuLaser network — the calculator caps it at 127 total modules, matching the protocol's addressing limit.

Why can't I put more than 8 detectors on one display?

That's a hardware limit of the ModuLaser protocol, not a calculator preference — the Cluster dropdown blocks the 9th assignment rather than just warning about it, whether the detectors are local or assigned from another page.

What happens if my page has detectors but no display?

The Cluster dropdown switches to listing clusters defined on other pages, so you can assign these detectors to a display module documented elsewhere. Once assigned, a note under the dropdown shows exactly which page and cluster it's on. A warning stays until every detector is assigned somewhere.

Why does the calculator warn about "disparate clusters"?

When a page has more than one cluster, each one needs its own dedicated AUX output — disparate clusters can't share power pathways, and their modules can't be ribbon-cabled together.

What are quiescent and AUX loads, and why are they shown separately?

They're inherent to the power supply itself, not the ModuLaser modules — quiescent is the supply's fixed baseline draw, AUX is your supervisory circuit load. The Total Cluster Load chart delineates them from module load for that reason.

Why is AUX capped at 4 circuits?

That's the hardware limit of a single APS6A/BPS6A power supply in built-in mode. Since each AUX circuit can now power only one entry point, this also means a sheet can have at most 4 entry points total. Third-party mode has no built-in cap since it depends on the third-party supply's own spec.

How does "Ribbon Assignment: Auto" decide which detectors ribbon-connect?

Auto (the default) fills each cluster's first entry point with up to 4 ribbon-connected followers, then automatically starts a second entry point once that's full — matching the display's 2-segment/4-follower ribbon limit exactly against the 8-detector cluster cap. It re-evaluates every detector on every recalculation, so it will override a manual choice you made earlier. Switch the toggle to Off if you want full manual control instead.

Can two entry points share the same AUX circuit?

No — each AUX circuit can power exactly one entry point per sheet. New entry points default to the first circuit nobody else is using; once a circuit is claimed, it's disabled ("taken") in every other entry point's dropdown, and can't be reassigned to another module until it's freed up. If a 5th entry point needs a circuit and all 4 are already taken, it's left unassigned with a flag calling it out — consolidate a module onto ribbon to free a circuit, or move it to another power supply page.

Why is Address a dropdown instead of free text, and why do options disappear?

Address is a 1–127 dropdown, unique across the entire network — not just the sheet you're on. Once a number is selected anywhere in the document, it shows disabled and labeled "(taken)" in every other module's Address dropdown, the same pattern used for AUX Circuit # and Cluster #. This prevents duplicate addresses from ever being entered in the first place, rather than just flagging them after the fact. Every module needs an address selected — a blank one keeps the sheet in INVALID CALCULATION.

Why is "Active AUX circuits" not editable?

It's counted automatically from how many distinct AUX circuits are actually assigned to entry points on the sheet, so it can never drift out of sync with the real wiring — add or remove an entry point and the count (and the AUX load it drives) updates immediately.

What are the AUX current limits, and what happens if I exceed them?

Each Edwards AUX output is rated 3.0 A. A 6 A supply (APS6A/BPS6A) totals 6.0 A across all circuits; a 10 A supply (APS10A/BPS10A) totals 8.0 A. Exceeding either triggers a Network Validation flag naming the affected circuit(s) — redistribute entry points across more circuits, or switch Power supply capacity to 10 A if that alone resolves it.

What's the difference between a yellow Validation Error and a red "INVALID CALCULATION" banner?

A yellow Validation Error flag means the math is still sound but something about the design isn't finished or clean yet — an empty cluster, a duplicate address, a ribbon segment over its limit, and so on. While any Validation Error is showing, the power supply/battery recommendation, the multi-supply recommendation, the Recommended Power Supply Arrangement, and the Bill of Materials are all withheld — resolve every flag to see them. The red INVALID CALCULATION banner is more severe: it means the sizing itself can't be trusted (required capacity exceeds the largest battery, a fan speed is unselected, or AUX current exceeds its hardware limit), and additionally marks the printed sheet conspicuously until fixed.

What does Auto Battery Sizing do?

In Edwards A/BPS mode (default Auto), it picks the battery size, Power supply capacity (6 A/10 A), and Battery cabinet together every time you recalculate — the smallest catalog battery that reaches 1 supply when achievable, switching to a 10 A supply only if total AUX draw needs the extra headroom, defaulting the cabinet to Edwards. In third-party custom mode (default Off, since you typically already have a manufacturer's spec in hand), switching it to Auto instead computes the battery Ah capacity from total load. The two toggles are independent — switching one doesn't affect the other. The controls Auto is driving gray out while it's active, since the calculator is choosing them for you; switch back to Off any time to enter them manually again. Auto only sizes the sheet in front of you; splitting clusters across multiple sheets is still done manually via Export additional arrangements.

Why do ribbon-connected modules move around on the sheet?

With Ribbon Assignment set to Auto, every ribbon-connected group (a display or detector entry point plus its followers) is kept sitting in consecutive rows automatically — moving an existing row relocates it in place, so nothing you've entered on that module is lost. Switch Ribbon Assignment to Off and the calculator stops moving rows for you; instead, a flag recommends moving a scattered ribbon group together yourself, or switching back to Auto to have it done automatically.

How does the third-party battery voltage field work?

Enter the actual voltage of the battery unit you're specifying (defaults to 12 VDC). The calculator works out how many units in series are needed to reach a 24 VDC nominal system — e.g., 2× for 12 V batteries, 4× for 6 V, 1× for 24 V — and reflects that series count in the Bill of Materials.

What's the NFPA 72 + NFPA 110 Generator Backup design basis?

These two presets (Non-Voice and Voice) use a 4-hour standby duration instead of the usual 24 hours, reflecting a design where a code-compliant emergency generator serves as secondary power rather than the battery alone. Confirm with your AHJ that a generator qualifies as secondary power for your specific installation before using this basis — not every jurisdiction or occupancy accepts it.

Why does a Battery cabinet choice appear for 50 Ah and 65 Ah batteries?

Those two sizes ship in a separate cabinet rather than the supply's own enclosure, and Edwards and Space Age both make a compatible option — Edwards 3-RCC7R (adding a 3-BATS shelf at 65 Ah) or Space Age SSU00507. Pick whichever your project standardizes on; it only changes the recommended part number, not the sizing math.

Why do I have to manually select a detector's fan speed?

Detector current draw depends on the airflow the detector is set to move, which comes from your pipe network design (PipeCAD) — there's no universally safe default, so the field starts blank. It's also one of the conditions that withholds the power supply/battery recommendation and the Bill of Materials until it's set.

What is the code compliance statement at the bottom of the sheet?

Where the selected Design Basis has a confirmed code citation, a green statement appears at the bottom of the sheet naming the exact standard, section, and paragraph the calculation is built to — e.g. NFPA 72 (2022 Edition), Section 10.6.7.2, or the relevant UFC 3-600-01 or BS 5839-1:2025 citation. It only appears when the sheet's calculation is fully clean — any Validation Error or INVALID CALCULATION condition hides it, since a compliance claim shouldn't be shown on a sheet with unresolved issues.

Why does BS 5839-1 use a different formula than everything else?

BS 5839-1:2025's own formula (Annex E) applies a derating factor to the alarm-period current specifically — Cmin = 1.25 × (standby Ah + 1.75 × alarm Ah) — rather than applying one factor uniformly like NFPA 72's formula does. Selecting any BS 5839-1 preset switches the sheet to this exact formula and locks Safety Margin to 25% (numerically the same as BS 5839-1's fixed 1.25 aging factor), so the two systems' calculations don't get mixed.

When should I use the Ambient Temperature selector, and what does NFPA 72 actually require here?

Battery capacity ratings assume 77°F/25°C. Use this selector when the battery's actual operating temperature — inside its enclosure, not the room at large — will deviate significantly from that: unheated enclosures, exterior cabinets, unconditioned mechanical rooms, and similar. Selecting a colder temperature applies a sizing multiplier (up to 1.61× at -10°C/14°F) to the required Ah before battery selection. It's set per sheet since different power supplies on the same project may sit in different environments.

What NFPA 72 requires, precisely: a 1.25 aging correction factor is mandatory — NFPA 72-2025 § 10.6.7.2.14 requires battery calculations to apply a minimum 1.25 factor so the battery can still meet the required load at the end of its service life, and this calculator applies it (as the Safety Margin, or as part of the BS 5839-1/CAN-ULC formula's own fixed factor). Temperature is different: NFPA 72 does not prescribe one universal temperature multiplier for every battery-sizing calculation. Annex A explains that correction factors address both aging and temperature effects, and the battery-capacity test procedure uses a temperature correction factor (KT) based on actual battery temperature — but per Annex A.14.4.3.2, that table is presented as part of the capacity-testing calculation, not as a single mandatory multiplier for initial sizing.

Practical implication: for initial sizing, the battery should be selected using the manufacturer's published capacity/discharge data at the anticipated operating temperature. Where the manufacturer provides temperature de-rating data or requires a temperature correction, apply that data — a battery rated at 77°F/25°C should not be assumed to deliver its full nameplate capacity at a substantially lower temperature. This calculator's temperature multiplier table is a reasonable general-guidance convenience, not itself a literal NFPA 72 mandate — where you have the specific battery manufacturer's own de-rating figures, those should take precedence over this calculator's generic table. In short: the 1.25 aging factor is required outright; temperature effects must be considered where they affect the battery's ability to meet the required duty cycle, normally using the manufacturer's own data. See NFPA 72-2025 § 10.6.7.2.14 and Annex A.14.4.3.2.

What does the "Print BOM" switch in the File menu do?

It's a single document-wide switch (not per-sheet) that leaves the Bill of Materials out of the PDF, Word and HTML output when unchecked. It is the same setting as the Bill of Materials box in the Print / Export dialog — changing one changes the other. The on-screen Bill of Materials, the Excel workbook and the Cluster Validation Report are unaffected. It's saved with the rest of the document, so it stays set the way you left it next time you open the file.

What does the Standalone / Networked addressing toggle do?

It's a per-sheet setting, just below the "Clusters supported" strip. Networked (the default, also for older files) uses addresses 1–127, unique across every Networked sheet — they form one SenseNET network. Standalone is for a sheet with no SenseNET pathway: addresses 1–9, unique within that sheet only. Since 02.00.004 a Standalone sheet may share addresses with any other sheet without a Validation Error; duplicates inside one Standalone sheet are still flagged. Cluster identification is still required either way.

Can the calculator number the addresses for me?

Yes (02.00.005). On a new build (first modules in an empty project) you pick Networked or Standalone, then Auto address? Yes / No. In an existing project, new sheets or modules (from a .pl import, + Add, or Merge) bring up three choices: Continue addressing per previous sheets (only the new ones, picking up after the last address; Standalone sheets start at 1), Manually address, or Re-auto address entire project. Each automatic change is logged and Undo (or Ctrl+Z) reverses it in one step. After a .pl import Confirm Settings? always comes first.

Why is "Incomplete" shown in amber with ⚠?

Since 02.00.005 every Incomplete indication — the sheet status (⚠ INCOMPLETE), an empty sheet's required Ah, battery and power-supply recommendation, the sidebar figure, the Live Results panel, the print stamp, summary tiles and tables, Word cells, the withheld power-supply recommendation, the print legend and the Cluster Validation Report's pending-validation chip — is amber with a warning symbol, in the same amber as the needs-entry outlines on screen and dark amber (#8a5a00 on #fdf3dc) in print and Word. Excel exports have no fills or colour (per Brent), so there it is plain text "⚠ Incomplete". The ⚠ and the word keep it readable in grayscale. Project files (.mlproj.json) still store the plain status.

What does the printed Cluster Validation Report look like?

Since 02.00.005 it prints in the KGS palette on white: a navy title bar, a project/network summary box, and per cluster a table with the entry point and Ribbon Path members marked by chips (Entry point, Ribbon Path), plus CALCULATION INVALID / VALIDATION ERROR PENDING chips on a sheet's BOM heading. Every page repeats a header (CLUSTER VALIDATION REPORT · project name · Network #) and a footer (app name and revision · Page n of N); table headers repeat across page breaks, rows are never split and cells don't wrap. Open it from View → Cluster Report and use its Print button.

Does the Excel CAD Schedule show standby, alarm and margin?

Yes (02.00.005). Each power supply table starts with a row giving that sheet's own Standby time in hours, Alarm time in minutes and Safety margin in percent.

My project mixes Networked and Standalone sheets — what should I do?

The amber Mixed addressing banner warns you. If it's intended (for example a standalone cluster in an outbuilding), leave it. Otherwise click Make all Networked… or Make all Standalone…; the confirm dialog offers Also re-address all modules (Networked: 1…N across all sheets in sheet order; Standalone: 1…n on each sheet). Only Address values (and, for Networked, each sheet's SenseNET network) change — module order, ribbon links, AUX and cluster assignments stay the same. Cancel changes nothing.

What happens when a design has more than 127 networked modules?

One SenseNET network supports 127 modules. The calculator adds SenseNET network 2 (then 3…) and moves whole sheets onto it — the last sheets in sheet order — addressing them from 1. A sheet never spans two networks. You're told a new network was added: make sure the design includes the infrastructure for it (its own SenseNET wiring/pathway and connection to the command display/FACP). Addresses only have to be unique within a network. Change a sheet's network by hand with the SenseNET network selector on its Addressing row.

What is "Show granular calculation detail," and where do I find it?

It's a per-sheet checkbox at the bottom of the results panel, below the Bill of Materials. Turning it on reveals a full formula breakdown for that sheet — module and supply load (including every individual module's own current draw, not just the Displays/Detectors subtotals), standby/alarm capacity, safety margin and temperature de-rating, and battery/supply sizing — each line showing both the formula and the actual numbers behind it, so the math is fully auditable rather than just the final figures. It's for sheets where that level of transparency is genuinely needed (an AHJ review, a second set of eyes checking the math), not something every sheet needs on by default. Off by default; saved per sheet with the rest of the document. For printouts and exports use the Detail level in File → Print / Export… instead: Standard adds the formulas with values, Full adds appendix pages after each sheet with every step (formula, values substituted, result, reference) — available in PDF, Word, HTML and the Excel Detail sheet whether or not this checkbox is on.

How does the battery size selection work?

Pick the Ah you want; the calculator rounds up to the nearest battery actually available (12V10A/17A/24A/40A/50A/65A, rated 10.5/18/26/40/50/65 Ah) and uses that model's real capacity for the sizing math and hardware recommendation — never a smaller battery than requested. In third-party mode, you define your own battery model and Ah capacity directly. Auto Battery Sizing does this for you automatically; see above.

What happens if my design needs more than one power supply?

The calculator groups your clusters and balances them across the number of supplies required, showing an independent load/Ah/hardware calculation for each — as long as no Validation Error is showing, since that recommendation is withheld otherwise. You can export the additional arrangements into their own location pages to document them separately.

What's the difference between built-in and third-party power supply mode?

Edwards A/BPS applies Edwards/EST's quiescent, AUX, hardware, and battery catalog automatically. Third-party lets you enter your own supply's quiescent/AUX/battery numbers, or skip straight to exporting the ModuLaser-only load (with its own Ah/supplies-required estimate) for use in that manufacturer's own calculator.

What is a ModuLaser project file (.mlproj.json)?

One file that both ModuLaser tools save. The Battery Calculator writes the design: project details, power supplies and batteries, detectors and displays with their addresses and 16-character names, and the PipeCAD pipe and sampling-hole data. The MASD Field Report writes the site results: inspections, configuration, tests and sign-off. Each tool keeps the other tool's part exactly as it found it, and neither replaces data without first showing you what would change. Use File → Export project… here and File → Import project… in the Field Report. Your normal Save As… calculator file still works the same way.

Why are Design Basis and Ambient Temperature amber on a new sheet?

They start on - Select - because there's no safe default: pick the code basis and the room temperature for this supply. Until both are chosen the sheet is INCOMPLETE (no de-rating is applied meanwhile, so no figures break). Files saved earlier keep their values. After a .pl import, Confirm Settings? applies the wizard's choices and marks them confirmed.

Will Validation Error flags stop me from finishing?

No — they don't stop you from editing, and you can keep working with flags showing. What they do withhold is the power supply/battery recommendation section, the multi-supply recommendation, the Recommended Power Supply Arrangement, and the Bill of Materials — all four stay blank until every flag clears. The INVALID CALCULATION banner works the same way but for its own three more severe conditions.
