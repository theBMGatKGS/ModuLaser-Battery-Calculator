Quick Start

Every document represents one (1) ModuLaser SenseNET network. Every sheet within a document represents one (1) power supply. A single power supply may serve more than one Cluster; different Clusters or Cluster segments must be powered by different circuits. The Cluster number is what ties modules together across sheets when a Cluster is distributed across multiple power supplies. A network has 1 Command Display Module, with up to 1 optional backup CDM for redundant SenseNET configurations.

A fully clean, valid calculation is required before a sheet's power supply/battery recommendations and Bill of Materials will generate. Steps 9–10 below cover the yellow Validation Error flags and the red INVALID CALCULATION banner, what triggers each, and how to fix them.

1
Fill in the project + network header

Every document represents one ModuLaser SenseNET network. Fill in Project Name, Prepared By, and Date plus SenseNET #, Building, and the Primary (and optional Backup) Command Display Module Location. A network is supported by 1 primary CDM plus up to 1 optional backup for redundant configurations — these fields identify where each is physically installed and carry into every printed sheet and the Cluster Validation Report.

2
Add a location per power supply, then name and locate it

Each location page = one power supply. Use "+ Add Location" to add another supply's sheet. Fill in the Power Supply Name (e.g. PS-1) and Power Supply Location (e.g. Riser Closet A) at the top of the location — both appear on the printed sheet header and in the Cluster Validation Report so every entry-point circuit can be traced back to a specific physical supply.

3
Pick a design basis

NFPA 72 Standard/Voice, NFPA 72 + NFPA 110 Generator Backup (Non-Voice/Voice), UFC 3-600-01 Hydro or FA/MNS, AWS, CAN/ULC-S524:2024 Standard/Voice, four BS 5839-1 categories (M/L, M/L + Generator, P Staffed, P Unstaffed), or Custom — sets Standby and Alarm duration. The generator-backup presets reflect a 4-hour standby basis where a code-compliant NFPA 110 generator serves as secondary power in place of the full 24-hour battery standby — confirm with your AHJ that a generator qualifies for your specific installation before relying on this basis. BS 5839-1 presets use that standard's own formula rather than the NFPA-72-style formula used everywhere else — its De-Rating factor is determined automatically (1 or 1.75, per Annex E's own rule) based on the selected battery, and the Safety Margin control locks to 25% automatically since that matches BS 5839-1's fixed aging factor. Category P (Unstaffed) is the one preset where Standby stays editable, capped at 72 hours. A new Ambient Temperature selector next to Alarm applies a de-rating multiplier when batteries won't sit at the standard 77°F/25°C — set per sheet, since different power supplies may be in different environments. When a design basis has a known code citation and the sheet's calculation is clean, a compliance statement citing the exact section/paragraph appears at the bottom of the sheet automatically.

4
Build each cluster

A Standard or Minimum Display starts a new cluster (numbered 01–63) and can host up to 8 Detectors. A Command Display works differently — it's network-wide rather than cluster-scoped (up to 2 per network), used for command/supervisory functions, and never hosts Detectors directly. Add Detectors and assign each to a cluster hosted by a Standard or Minimum Display. Detectors on a display-less sheet can reference clusters hosted on other sheets — and detectors on that same sheet sharing that same remote reference can still ribbon-connect to each other locally, forming their own portion of the cluster's ribbon topology (up to the usual 2-segment limit for that sheet's contribution), while linking back to the rest of the cluster over SNET+ rather than ribbon.

5
Address, Zone, Power, Circuit source

Give each module an Address (a dropdown of 1–127, unique across the entire network — once claimed on any sheet, it's disabled everywhere else) and a Zone descriptor. Every module needs an address; leaving one blank keeps the sheet in INVALID CALCULATION until it's set. Displays are always entry points from a circuit. By default, Ribbon Assignment (the toggle next to "MASD Modules") is set to Auto — new detectors automatically ribbon-connect to the first entry point in their cluster with room, and once a cluster's ribbon capacity is full (4 followers per entry point, 2 entry points per cluster), the next detector automatically becomes a new entry point. Auto also keeps every ribbon-connected group of modules sitting in consecutive rows on the sheet, moving them together automatically as clusters change — nothing you've entered on a module is lost when it moves, since the row itself relocates rather than being recreated. Switch it to Off to assign Power manually instead — Auto will stop re-evaluating your choices and stop reordering rows while it's off, though a flag will still recommend moving ribbon-connected modules together if they end up scattered. Each entry point also gets an AUX Circuit # (AUX 01–04) — new entry points default to a circuit nobody else is using yet, and once a circuit is claimed by one entry point, it's locked out for every other entry point on that sheet — a sheet has at most 4 entry points, since there are only 4 physical circuits. If a 5th entry point needs one and all 4 are already taken, it's left unassigned with a flag calling it out — free up a circuit (consolidate a module onto ribbon) or move it to another power supply page. Enter Circuit source on every entry point (e.g., PS-1 AUX-2).

6
Choose Networked or Standalone addressing

Just below "Clusters supported" is a per-sheet Networked / Standalone toggle. Networked is the default (unchanged for every existing project) — Address stays the 1–127 dropdown from Step 5, unique across the whole document. Standalone is for a cluster with no SenseNET pathway connecting it to anything else: Address becomes a 1–9 dropdown, and uniqueness is scoped to that one cluster rather than the whole document — a completely unrelated cluster elsewhere can freely reuse the same addresses, even if it happens to share the same cluster number, since there's no physical network tying them together. A sheet with multiple clusters still gets an independent 1–9 pool per cluster even in Standalone mode, and Cluster identification is still fully required either way — Standalone only changes the addressing pool, nothing else. Cross-sheet cluster referencing (Step 4) keeps working normally no matter which scheme either sheet uses. Switching a sheet to Standalone clears any of its own addresses already set above 9, since they're no longer valid options.

7
Fan speed and supply mode

Set each detector's fan speed from PipeCAD. Pick Edwards A/BPS or third-party (custom loads / export ModuLaser loads only). Third-party custom mode has its own Auto Battery Sizing toggle, independent from built-in mode's — it defaults to Off so you can enter the Ah rating from your manufacturer's spec directly, with Auto available if you'd rather have it computed from total load. Enter your battery's actual voltage and the calculator works out how many units in series reach the 24 VDC nominal system target.

8
AUX + battery size

Auto Battery Sizing (the toggle above Required Battery Capacity, default Auto) picks battery size, Power supply capacity, and Battery cabinet together from your total load — the smallest catalog size that reaches 1 supply when possible, switching to 10 A only if AUX draw needs it. Switch it to Off to set all three manually instead. Edwards AUX outputs are rated 3.0 A per circuit, 6.0 A total on a 6 A supply (APS6A/BPS6A) or 8.0 A total on a 10 A supply (APS10A/BPS10A). For 50 Ah and 65 Ah batteries, a Battery cabinet choice appears — Edwards 3-RCC7R (with 3-BATS shelf at 65 Ah) or Space Age SSU00507.

9
Review Validation Error flags

Yellow-highlighted Validation Error flags call out detector/display mismatches, ribbon-segment size limits, duplicate addresses, missing circuit sources, and AUX coverage. They don't block editing, but the power supply/battery recommendation section, the multi-supply recommendation, the Recommended Power Supply Arrangement, and the Bill of Materials are all withheld until every flag clears — including a missing fan speed selection. Resolve everything shown here before expecting a hardware recommendation at the bottom of the sheet.

10
Resolve any INVALID CALCULATION banner

Separate from — and more severe than — a Validation Error flag, a bold red "INVALID CALCULATION" banner at the top of a sheet means the numbers themselves can't be trusted. It fires on any of four conditions, each with a specific fix:

  • Required battery capacity exceeds 65 Ah (the largest available battery) — increase the battery size where possible, or split the location's clusters across additional power supply sheets using the Recommended Power Supply Arrangement below.
  • A detector has no fan speed selected — select every detector's fan speed from your PipeCAD calculation. The current draw shown is incomplete until every fan speed is set.
  • A module has no Address selected — pick an address (1–127) for every module on the sheet.
  • AUX current exceeds the hardware limit (3.0 A per circuit, or 6.0/8.0 A total) — redistribute entry points across more AUX circuits, or switch Power supply capacity to 10 A if that alone resolves it.

The banner — and the red border and diagonal watermark that come with it in print — clear automatically the moment every condition is resolved.

11
Multi-supply arrangements

If more than one supply is required, check the Recommended Power Supply Arrangement and use "Export additional arrangements" to split them onto their own sheets. Cluster numbers stay the same across sheets — the Cluster Report button in the toolbar ties everything back together.

12
Save your work and export results

Save As… in the toolbar downloads the whole document — every sheet, every field — as a single self-contained .html file you can keep, email, or reopen later in any browser; opening it restores the document exactly as saved. The Autosave toggle (next to it) is a convenience on top of that: once turned on, it saves your current work automatically to this browser's local storage as you go, so a reload doesn't lose it — but it's tied to this specific browser and device, not portable like a saved file, so it's not a substitute for an occasional Save As when you want a real, shareable copy. Export to Excel in the toolbar produces a multi-sheet .xlsx workbook, one worksheet per power supply, mirroring each sheet's module table and results — useful for handing data to someone who wants it outside this tool.

Standards and Formulas

General reference only — not a substitute for the current adopted edition of NFPA 72, your local building/fire code, the AHJ's requirements, or the equipment manufacturer's published installation instructions. Code editions and local amendments change; always verify against what's actually adopted for your jurisdiction and project.

Every Design Basis preset in this calculator resolves to one of the battery-sizing formulas below. This section names the reference standard behind each preset and shows the exact formula used, so the math can be checked against the source document directly.

NFPA 72 (2022 Edition), Section 10.6.7.2

Governs the Standard, Voice, and both NFPA 110 Generator Backup presets. Also the underlying formula for AWS and both UFC 3-600-01 presets — see below.

Required Capacity (Ah) = (Istandby × Tstandby + Ialarm × Talarm) × 1.25

Tstandby = 24 h (or 4 h where an NFPA 110-compliant generator serves as secondary power) · Talarm = 5 min (standard) or 15 min (voice/mass notification) · 1.25 = the standard's aging/safety factor for the 2022 edition and later (editions before 2022 used 1.20 — this calculator's NFPA 72 Edition selector reflects that difference as the minimum Safety Margin). This 1.25 aging factor is mandatory (NFPA 72-2025 § 10.6.7.2.14) — it is separate from temperature effects, which NFPA 72 does not reduce to a single mandatory sizing multiplier; see the FAQ entry on the Ambient Temperature selector for the full distinction.

UFC 3-600-01, Fire Protection Engineering for Facilities (8 August 2016, w/ Change 6 dated 6 May 2021)

Uses the same NFPA 72 formula above, with its own standby/alarm durations specified for two distinct scenarios:

Required Capacity (Ah) = (Istandby × Tstandby + Ialarm × Talarm) × 1.25

Hydro (¶4-24.7): Hydroelectric Power Plant voice evacuation — follows NFPA 72's in-building voice evacuation provisions: Tstandby = 24 h, Talarm = 15 min. FA/MNS (¶9-18.11.3): Fire Alarm and Mass Notification System — Tstandby = 48 h, Talarm = 15 min.

AWS Specification 283116, 25H1B

Not a distinct sizing standard of its own — AWS cites NFPA 72's own requirements (Section 10.6.7.2, same formula as above), with the Owner specifying an enhanced alarm period beyond the code minimum:

Required Capacity (Ah) = (Istandby × Tstandby + Ialarm × Talarm) × 1.25

Tstandby = 24 h (NFPA 72 code minimum, unchanged) · Talarm = 120 min — an Owner-driven enhancement in place of NFPA 72's 5/15-minute code minimum, reflecting mission-critical reliability expectations beyond baseline code requirements. Also compliant with NFPA 855.

CAN/ULC-S524:2024

Canadian standard for fire alarm system installation. Structurally the same formula shape as NFPA 72, with its own 1.25 safety factor introduced specifically in the 2024 edition (not present in earlier editions in the same form). Not yet adopted into the National Building Code of Canada as of this writing, though already referenced by the Ontario Fire Code — confirm what's actually adopted in your jurisdiction.

Required Capacity (Ah) = (Istandby × Tstandby + Ialarm × Talarm) × 1.25

Tstandby = 24 h · Talarm = 5 min (Standard) or 15 min (Voice, where voice communication is installed) · 1.25 = the fixed safety factor this calculator applies for both presets (shown via the Safety Margin selector, locked to 25% while a CAN/ULC-S524:2024 preset is active — this calculator does not have independent confirmation of a distinct margin requirement in editions before 2024, so it doesn't attempt to model one).

BS 5839-1:2025, Annex E

A materially different formula from NFPA 72 — applies a derating factor (D) to the alarm term specifically, rather than one factor uniformly. Governs all four BS 5839-1 presets in this calculator.

Cmin = 1.25 × (I1 × T1 + D × I2 × T2)

I1×T1 = standby term · I2×T2 = alarm term · D = 1 or 1.75, determined automatically by comparing the selected battery's 20-hour rate to the alarm current (see the De-Rating factor field and the Battery and Charger Sizing entry below for the full determination rule) · 1.25 = fixed aging factor (shown via this calculator's Safety Margin, locked to 25% while a BS 5839-1 preset is active). Standby/alarm durations by category: M (Manual) or L (Life Safety) = 24 h / 30 min · M/L with generator = 6 h / 30 min · P (staffed/ARC-monitored) = 24 h / 30 min · P (unstaffed) = 24 h + unoccupied period, up to 72 h total / 30 min.

Other Code Requirements
Listing requirements

ModuLaser requires "UL Listed," "FM Approved," or other NRTL-specific certification depending on the requirements of the local authority having jurisdiction (LAHJ) — the acceptable framework isn't the same everywhere. Consult the ModuLaser manual for your region to determine which certification framework applies before selecting a power supply.

Compatibility listing — UL regions

In UL regions, any power supply listed under category UOJZ or UTRZ is suitable for use with ModuLaser, provided both the power supply and the ModuLaser modules are installed and configured strictly in accordance with each manufacturer's published instructions. Deviating from either manufacturer's published instructions — even with a UOJZ/UTRZ-listed supply — can invalidate the installation's compliance.

Reserve capacity (secondary power / battery standby)

NFPA 72 requires secondary power (typically batteries) sized to supply the system for a specified standby period, then still carry the full alarm load for a specified alarm period — commonly 24 hours standby + 5 minutes alarm for a standard system, or 24 hours + 15 minutes where voice/mass notification is involved. This calculator's Design Basis presets encode exactly these standby/alarm combinations. Where a code-compliant emergency or standby generator serves as secondary power in place of full battery standby, NFPA 110 governs the generator itself and the standby duration may be reduced (commonly to 4 hours) — the NFPA 72 + NFPA 110 Generator Backup presets reflect this, but confirm with your AHJ that a generator is accepted as secondary power for your specific occupancy and installation before relying on the reduced duration.

Battery and charger sizing margin

Both standards this calculator supports apply a similar aging/safety margin, typically 20% to 25% of the raw standby + alarm Ah calculation: NFPA 72 uses 1.25 (25%) in the 2022 edition and later, or 1.20 (20%) in editions before 2022 — this calculator's NFPA 72 Edition selector sets the applicable minimum accordingly. BS 5839-1:2025 uses a fixed 1.25 (25%) aging factor, accounting for roughly 5% capacity degradation per year over a 4-year assumed battery life. This calculator's Safety Margin selector applies the percentage on top of the raw Ah requirement (locked to 25% and disabled while a BS 5839-1 preset is active, matching its fixed factor); the AH per AUX Circuit and Total AUX Current checks separately confirm the supply's charger and output circuits aren't overloaded independent of battery sizing.

BS 5839-1:2025 battery de-rating factor (D)

In BS 5839-1:2025 Annex E (reorganized and clarified from Annex D/E in the 2017 edition — if updating from 2017, confirm your calculations use the new formula and D-factor application), the battery de-rating factor D accounts for reduced battery capacity when discharged at high alarm currents (the Peukert effect). It's applied in the standby battery capacity formula: Cmin = 1.25 × [(I1×T1) + D×(I2×T2)], where I1/T1 are the standby current/period, I2/T2 are the alarm current/period (typically 0.5 h), and 1.25 is the aging factor described above.

How D is determined: if the battery's 20-hour rate capacity (Ah rating ÷ 20, giving a sustained current in Amps) meets or exceeds the alarm current, D = 1. Otherwise, D = 1.75 (or a higher manufacturer-specified value). D = 1 is rare for fire alarm batteries in practice, since alarm loads are typically high relative to available battery capacity — D = 1.75 is the typical default for VRLA batteries in fire alarm systems. This calculator determines D automatically: it compares the currently selected battery's 20-hour rate against the sheet's total load (used as the alarm current in this calculator's model), and applies D = 1 only when that battery genuinely qualifies — otherwise D = 1.75. You can still manually enter a higher value if your battery or panel manufacturer specifies one, but the calculator won't let you enter a value below whichever the standard determines for the battery actually selected. Always confirm against the panel manufacturer's data and the battery manufacturer's specifications; failing to apply the correct D can lead to undersized batteries and non-compliance.

Circuit and output ratings

Every notification, initiating, and auxiliary output circuit on a listed power supply has a maximum rated current that must not be exceeded, and the supply's total output across all circuits combined has its own separate maximum. This calculator enforces the Edwards-published 3.0 A per AUX circuit and 6.0 A (6 A supply) / 8.0 A (10 A supply) total limits as part of its Validation Error checks — exceeding either is a real installation defect, not just a calculator formality.

Wiring and pathway survivability

Depending on occupancy type and the specific circuits involved, NFPA 72 may require initiating device circuits, notification appliance circuits, or signaling line circuits to use a specific pathway survivability level (Class N through Class X, per NFPA 72's pathway classification), which affects wiring topology, supervision, and fault tolerance — separate from, but related to, the ribbon/circuit source assignments tracked in this calculator's Power column and Cluster Validation Report.

Documentation for the AHJ

Most AHJs expect battery calculations, device compatibility, and circuit loading to be documented and available for plan review and final inspection. The printed sheet, Cluster Validation Report, and Bill of Materials this calculator produces are meant to support that documentation — but the calculator itself doesn't submit to or track approval from any AHJ, and its output should be reviewed by a qualified, licensed design professional before submission.

FAQs

What's a "cluster"?

A Standard or Minimum Display Module (FHSD8310-ULF / FHSD8300-ULF) plus up to 8 Detector Modules (FHSD8330-ULF) wired to it. The display is what makes a cluster a cluster — detectors alone have nowhere to report.

What's the difference between Standard, Minimum, and Command Display?

Standard and Minimum Displays each host one cluster of up to 8 detectors. A Command Display is network-wide — it shows status across the whole network but doesn't host detectors itself, and only 2 are allowed per network.

What is a "network" in this calculator?

Each location page represents one ModuLaser network — the calculator caps it at 127 total modules, matching the protocol's addressing limit.

Why can't I put more than 8 detectors on one display?

That's a hardware limit of the ModuLaser protocol, not a calculator preference — the Cluster dropdown blocks the 9th assignment rather than just warning about it, whether the detectors are local or assigned from another page.

What happens if my page has detectors but no display?

The Cluster dropdown switches to listing clusters defined on other pages, so you can assign these detectors to a display module documented elsewhere. Once assigned, a note under the dropdown shows exactly which page and cluster it's on. A warning stays until every detector is assigned somewhere.

Why does the calculator warn about "disparate clusters"?

When a page has more than one cluster, each one needs its own dedicated AUX output — disparate clusters can't share power pathways, and their modules can't be ribbon-cabled together.

What are quiescent and AUX loads, and why are they shown separately?

They're inherent to the power supply itself, not the ModuLaser modules — quiescent is the supply's fixed baseline draw, AUX is your supervisory circuit load. The Total Cluster Load chart delineates them from module load for that reason.

Why is AUX capped at 4 circuits?

That's the hardware limit of a single APS6A/BPS6A power supply in built-in mode. Since each AUX circuit can now power only one entry point, this also means a sheet can have at most 4 entry points total. Third-party mode has no built-in cap since it depends on the third-party supply's own spec.

How does "Ribbon Assignment: Auto" decide which detectors ribbon-connect?

Auto (the default) fills each cluster's first entry point with up to 4 ribbon-connected followers, then automatically starts a second entry point once that's full — matching the display's 2-segment/4-follower ribbon limit exactly against the 8-detector cluster cap. It re-evaluates every detector on every recalculation, so it will override a manual choice you made earlier. Switch the toggle to Off if you want full manual control instead.

Can two entry points share the same AUX circuit?

No — each AUX circuit can power exactly one entry point per sheet. New entry points default to the first circuit nobody else is using; once a circuit is claimed, it's disabled ("taken") in every other entry point's dropdown, and can't be reassigned to another module until it's freed up. If a 5th entry point needs a circuit and all 4 are already taken, it's left unassigned with a flag calling it out — consolidate a module onto ribbon to free a circuit, or move it to another power supply page.

Why is Address a dropdown instead of free text, and why do options disappear?

Address is a 1–127 dropdown, unique across the entire network — not just the sheet you're on. Once a number is selected anywhere in the document, it shows disabled and labeled "(taken)" in every other module's Address dropdown, the same pattern used for AUX Circuit # and Cluster #. This prevents duplicate addresses from ever being entered in the first place, rather than just flagging them after the fact. Every module needs an address selected — a blank one keeps the sheet in INVALID CALCULATION.

Why is "Active AUX circuits" not editable?

It's counted automatically from how many distinct AUX circuits are actually assigned to entry points on the sheet, so it can never drift out of sync with the real wiring — add or remove an entry point and the count (and the AUX load it drives) updates immediately.

What are the AUX current limits, and what happens if I exceed them?

Each Edwards AUX output is rated 3.0 A. A 6 A supply (APS6A/BPS6A) totals 6.0 A across all circuits; a 10 A supply (APS10A/BPS10A) totals 8.0 A. Exceeding either triggers a Network Validation flag naming the affected circuit(s) — redistribute entry points across more circuits, or switch Power supply capacity to 10 A if that alone resolves it.

What's the difference between a yellow Validation Error and a red "INVALID CALCULATION" banner?

A yellow Validation Error flag means the math is still sound but something about the design isn't finished or clean yet — an empty cluster, a duplicate address, a ribbon segment over its limit, and so on. While any Validation Error is showing, the power supply/battery recommendation, the multi-supply recommendation, the Recommended Power Supply Arrangement, and the Bill of Materials are all withheld — resolve every flag to see them. The red INVALID CALCULATION banner is more severe: it means the sizing itself can't be trusted (required capacity exceeds the largest battery, a fan speed is unselected, or AUX current exceeds its hardware limit), and additionally marks the printed sheet conspicuously until fixed.

What does Auto Battery Sizing do?

In Edwards A/BPS mode (default Auto), it picks the battery size, Power supply capacity (6 A/10 A), and Battery cabinet together every time you recalculate — the smallest catalog battery that reaches 1 supply when achievable, switching to a 10 A supply only if total AUX draw needs the extra headroom, defaulting the cabinet to Edwards. In third-party custom mode (default Off, since you typically already have a manufacturer's spec in hand), switching it to Auto instead computes the battery Ah capacity from total load. The two toggles are independent — switching one doesn't affect the other. The controls Auto is driving gray out while it's active, since the calculator is choosing them for you; switch back to Off any time to enter them manually again. Auto only sizes the sheet in front of you; splitting clusters across multiple sheets is still done manually via Export additional arrangements.

Why do ribbon-connected modules move around on the sheet?

With Ribbon Assignment set to Auto, every ribbon-connected group (a display or detector entry point plus its followers) is kept sitting in consecutive rows automatically — moving an existing row relocates it in place, so nothing you've entered on that module is lost. Switch Ribbon Assignment to Off and the calculator stops moving rows for you; instead, a flag recommends moving a scattered ribbon group together yourself, or switching back to Auto to have it done automatically.

How does the third-party battery voltage field work?

Enter the actual voltage of the battery unit you're specifying (defaults to 12 VDC). The calculator works out how many units in series are needed to reach a 24 VDC nominal system — e.g., 2× for 12 V batteries, 4× for 6 V, 1× for 24 V — and reflects that series count in the Bill of Materials.

What's the NFPA 72 + NFPA 110 Generator Backup design basis?

These two presets (Non-Voice and Voice) use a 4-hour standby duration instead of the usual 24 hours, reflecting a design where a code-compliant emergency generator serves as secondary power rather than the battery alone. Confirm with your AHJ that a generator qualifies as secondary power for your specific installation before using this basis — not every jurisdiction or occupancy accepts it.

Why does a Battery cabinet choice appear for 50 Ah and 65 Ah batteries?

Those two sizes ship in a separate cabinet rather than the supply's own enclosure, and Edwards and Space Age both make a compatible option — Edwards 3-RCC7R (adding a 3-BATS shelf at 65 Ah) or Space Age SSU00507. Pick whichever your project standardizes on; it only changes the recommended part number, not the sizing math.

Why do I have to manually select a detector's fan speed?

Detector current draw depends on the airflow the detector is set to move, which comes from your pipe network design (PipeCAD) — there's no universally safe default, so the field starts blank. It's also one of the conditions that withholds the power supply/battery recommendation and the Bill of Materials until it's set.

What is the code compliance statement at the bottom of the sheet?

Where the selected Design Basis has a confirmed code citation, a green statement appears at the bottom of the sheet naming the exact standard, section, and paragraph the calculation is built to — e.g. NFPA 72 (2022 Edition), Section 10.6.7.2, or the relevant UFC 3-600-01 or BS 5839-1:2025 citation. It only appears when the sheet's calculation is fully clean — any Validation Error or INVALID CALCULATION condition hides it, since a compliance claim shouldn't be shown on a sheet with unresolved issues.

Why does BS 5839-1 use a different formula than everything else?

BS 5839-1:2025's own formula (Annex E) applies a derating factor to the alarm-period current specifically — Cmin = 1.25 × (standby Ah + 1.75 × alarm Ah) — rather than applying one factor uniformly like NFPA 72's formula does. Selecting any BS 5839-1 preset switches the sheet to this exact formula and locks Safety Margin to 25% (numerically the same as BS 5839-1's fixed 1.25 aging factor), so the two systems' calculations don't get mixed.

When should I use the Ambient Temperature selector, and what does NFPA 72 actually require here?

Battery capacity ratings assume 77°F/25°C. Use this selector when the battery's actual operating temperature — inside its enclosure, not the room at large — will deviate significantly from that: unheated enclosures, exterior cabinets, unconditioned mechanical rooms, and similar. Selecting a colder temperature applies a sizing multiplier (up to 1.61× at -10°C/14°F) to the required Ah before battery selection. It's set per sheet since different power supplies on the same project may sit in different environments.

What NFPA 72 requires, precisely: a 1.25 aging correction factor is mandatory — NFPA 72-2025 § 10.6.7.2.14 requires battery calculations to apply a minimum 1.25 factor so the battery can still meet the required load at the end of its service life, and this calculator applies it (as the Safety Margin, or as part of the BS 5839-1/CAN-ULC formula's own fixed factor). Temperature is different: NFPA 72 does not prescribe one universal temperature multiplier for every battery-sizing calculation. Annex A explains that correction factors address both aging and temperature effects, and the battery-capacity test procedure uses a temperature correction factor (KT) based on actual battery temperature — but per Annex A.14.4.3.2, that table is presented as part of the capacity-testing calculation, not as a single mandatory multiplier for initial sizing.

Practical implication: for initial sizing, the battery should be selected using the manufacturer's published capacity/discharge data at the anticipated operating temperature. Where the manufacturer provides temperature de-rating data or requires a temperature correction, apply that data — a battery rated at 77°F/25°C should not be assumed to deliver its full nameplate capacity at a substantially lower temperature. This calculator's temperature multiplier table is a reasonable general-guidance convenience, not itself a literal NFPA 72 mandate — where you have the specific battery manufacturer's own de-rating figures, those should take precedence over this calculator's generic table. In short: the 1.25 aging factor is required outright; temperature effects must be considered where they affect the battery's ability to meet the required duty cycle, normally using the manufacturer's own data. See NFPA 72-2025 § 10.6.7.2.14 and Annex A.14.4.3.2.

What does the "Print BOM" toggle in the toolbar do?

It's a single document-wide switch (not per-sheet) that leaves the Bill of Materials out of the printed/PDF output entirely when unchecked — every sheet's BOM, and its identifying header, are skipped. It only affects print; the on-screen Bill of Materials, Excel export, and Cluster Validation Report are unaffected. It's saved with the rest of the document, so it stays set the way you left it next time you open the file.

What does the Standalone / Networked addressing toggle do?

It's a per-sheet setting, just below the "Clusters supported" strip, controlling how Address is scoped. Networked (the default, both for new sheets and for any project saved before this feature existed) is today's familiar behavior: Address is a 1–127 dropdown, unique across the entire document — once claimed anywhere, it's disabled/"(taken)" everywhere else. Standalone is for a cluster with no SenseNET pathway connecting it to anything else: Address becomes a 1–9 dropdown, and uniqueness is scoped to that specific cluster rather than the whole document — a different, unrelated cluster elsewhere (even one that happens to share the same cluster number) can freely reuse the same addresses, since there's no physical network tying them together. If a sheet has multiple clusters, each cluster still gets its own independent 1–9 pool, and Cluster identification is still fully required either way — Standalone only changes the addressing pool, nothing else. Cross-sheet cluster referencing keeps working normally regardless of either sheet's addressing scheme. Switching a sheet to Standalone clears any of its own addresses above 9, since they're no longer valid options.

What is "Show granular calculation detail," and where do I find it?

It's a per-sheet checkbox at the bottom of the results panel, below the Bill of Materials. Turning it on reveals a full formula breakdown for that sheet — module and supply load (including every individual module's own current draw, not just the Displays/Detectors subtotals), standby/alarm capacity, safety margin and temperature de-rating, and battery/supply sizing — each line showing both the formula and the actual numbers behind it, so the math is fully auditable rather than just the final figures. It's for sheets where that level of transparency is genuinely needed (an AHJ review, a second set of eyes checking the math), not something every sheet needs on by default. When it's on, that sheet prints an additional page (after the calculation sheet and Bill of Materials) containing the same breakdown — it never affects whether the calculation sheet itself fits on one page, since the detail page is entirely separate. Off by default; saved per sheet with the rest of the document.

How does the battery size selection work?

Pick the Ah you want; the calculator rounds up to the nearest battery actually available (12V10A/17A/24A/40A/50A/65A, rated 10.5/18/26/40/50/65 Ah) and uses that model's real capacity for the sizing math and hardware recommendation — never a smaller battery than requested. In third-party mode, you define your own battery model and Ah capacity directly. Auto Battery Sizing does this for you automatically; see above.

What happens if my design needs more than one power supply?

The calculator groups your clusters and balances them across the number of supplies required, showing an independent load/Ah/hardware calculation for each — as long as no Validation Error is showing, since that recommendation is withheld otherwise. You can export the additional arrangements into their own location pages to document them separately.

What's the difference between built-in and third-party power supply mode?

Edwards A/BPS applies Edwards/EST's quiescent, AUX, hardware, and battery catalog automatically. Third-party lets you enter your own supply's quiescent/AUX/battery numbers, or skip straight to exporting the ModuLaser-only load (with its own Ah/supplies-required estimate) for use in that manufacturer's own calculator.

Will Validation Error flags stop me from finishing?

No — they don't stop you from editing, and you can keep working with flags showing. What they do withhold is the power supply/battery recommendation section, the multi-supply recommendation, the Recommended Power Supply Arrangement, and the Bill of Materials — all four stay blank until every flag clears. The INVALID CALCULATION banner works the same way but for its own three more severe conditions.
